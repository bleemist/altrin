Foundational Genesis

The ALTRIN Story: From Crisis Response to Sustained Impact

The Albertine Transformation Initiative (ALTRIN) was born out of an urgent community imperative in late 2021. Following more than two years of unprecedented school closures during the global COVID-19 pandemic, schools across Uganda faced an alarming surge in student dropouts, learning loss, and household economic dislocation. In Buliisa District and the surrounding Albertine region, vulnerable learners were rapidly drifting away from formal education.

Recognizing that temporary aid could not solve systemic educational collapse, Founding Director **Mr. Henry Busobozi** together with founding members including **Mr. David Bingi** and a team of passionate educators initiated a ground-level **Back to School Survey** in December 2021. They visited primary and secondary school directors, parents, and community leaders to evaluate school readiness, assess household financial distress, and encourage student re-enrolment.

What began as a localized rapid-assessment campaign quickly evolved into a structured institutional movement. The survey revealed three fundamental truths:

  1. Academic dropouts are predictable and preventable if student enrolment and attendance are systematically tracked from Primary 1 to University graduation.
  2. Child education cannot be sustained without parental financial security, requiring direct household economic empowerment programs.
  3. Youth motivation thrives when academic learning is paired with leadership galas, debate competitions, career guidance, and environmental climate stewardship.

Building upon these pillars, ALTRIN formally launched its flagship **ALTRIN Week** in October 2023 at the Biiso Sub-County Community Hall, uniting schools across the region. Today, ALTRIN stands as a robust multi-sectoral initiative impacting over 500 learners, 10 partner schools, and 20 educators across Western Uganda.

Our Operational & Monitoring Philosophy

ALTRIN operates with total transparency, field accountability, and data-driven oversight. Every project is continuously monitored by our dedicated Monitoring, Evaluation, and Learning (MEL) department led by **Mr. Ahebwa Clinton** and MEL Officers **Mr. Asingwire Daniel** and **Ms. Namubiru Catherine**. Field data feeds directly into our program managers and compliance team, ensuring that every shilling invested produces tangible educational and community outcomes.

Institutional Standards & Roadmap

Governance Integrity, Child Safeguarding, & Strategic Horizon 2026–2030

Child Safeguarding & Learner Data Ethics

Because ALTRIN operates directly within primary and secondary school environments across Buliisa District, institutional integrity and child protection are non-negotiable pillars. Overseen by Programs & Compliance Manager **Ms. Atugonza Doreen** and Legal Advisor **Mr. Muliisa John Dosman**, our operational framework enforces strict Child Safeguarding Protocols across all field activities.

All learner data collected under the **Learner Tracking System (LTS)** is encrypted and managed under strict parental consent and institutional data privacy guidelines. We ensure that student academic records, home visitation logs, and financial risk profiles are accessible exclusively to authorized MEL officers for counseling and educational support.

Strategic Expansion Roadmap (2026–2030)

ALTRIN’s strategic plan for 2026–2030 focuses on expanding institutional capacity and broadening regional impact across Western Uganda. Guided by our Executive Leadership, our key targets include:

  • Expanding partner school coverage from 10+ to 35+ primary & secondary schools.
  • Establishing permanent TaRL teacher training hubs in Buliisa District.
  • Scaling Parent VSLA micro-loan groups to cover 1,500+ rural households.
  • Planting 50,000 indigenous trees through school eco-clubs by 2030.

National & International Policy Alignment

ALTRIN’s multi-sectoral model directly aligns with Uganda’s **National Development Plan (NDP III)** for Human Capital Development, the Ministry of Education & Sports primary retention framework, and the United Nations **Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)**—specifically **SDG 4 (Quality Education)**, **SDG 1 (No Poverty)**, **SDG 8 (Decent Work)**, and **SDG 13 (Climate Action)**.
